# Memex

The **memex** (a portmanteau of "memory" and "index") is a theoretical proto-hypertext system described by Vannevar Bush in his influential 1945 essay *"As We May Think"*, published in The Atlantic Monthly.

## What is the Memex?

Bush envisioned the memex as a device in which an individual could store all their books, records, and communications, mechanically indexed and organized so that information could be consulted with exceeding speed and flexibility. It would be an enlarged intimate supplement to one's memory.

### Key Features

- **Storage**: Could hold vast amounts of information on microfilm
- **Linking**: Users could create "trails" of associated information, linking related concepts together
- **Indexing**: Sophisticated mechanical indexing for quick retrieval
- **Personal Knowledge Base**: A private, extensible repository of knowledge
- **Associative Thinking**: Mimicked the way the human mind makes associations

## Historical Significance

The memex was never built, but its conceptual influence has been profound:

1. **Hypertext**: Ted Nelson and Douglas Engelbart later developed hypertext systems inspired by Bush's vision
2. **World Wide Web**: Tim Berners-Lee cited the memex as an inspiration for creating the web
3. **Personal Knowledge Management**: Modern tools like Obsidian, Roam Research, and Notion reflect memex ideals
4. **Information Retrieval**: The concept influenced modern search engines and database systems

## Legacy

> "Consider a future device [...] in which an individual stores all his books, records, and communications, and which is mechanized so that it may be consulted with exceeding speed and flexibility."
> — Vannevar Bush, "As We May Think," 1945

The memex remains a touchstone for thinking about how humans can better organize, connect, and retrieve information. Its vision of associative linking and personal knowledge management continues to inspire new tools and technologies.
